## The story

It is the 31st year of King Hui of Liang. The State of Liang is losing the war against Qin, and the recruiters have taken every man who can hold a spear. They would have taken you too, but the sergeant saw the burn scars on your forearms: *"A smith. The front has enough corpses. The army needs blades."*

So you are kept back in a supply camp behind the lines, mending the weapons that come back from the fighting, while your wife, your daughter and your little son wait for you in Willow Bend. Serve your days without failing the quota too often, and the army lets you go home on leave. What you find at home, and what you choose to do about it, decides where you forge next: for the army, for outlaws in the Reed Marsh, in the gilded cage of a corrupt magistrate's armory, or in the Royal Arsenal itself.

## How to forge

Each duty day you get **three broken weapons** and a **quota** in wen (copper coins).

1. **Heat it once.** Hold the bellows and let go when the gauge is in the orange band. The heat decides how many strikes you get (about ten). The pale centre of the band gives extra strikes. Push it into the white and the blade cracks, which makes it more likely to shatter.
2. **Strike on the anvil.** A marker sweeps across the timing bar. Every strike rolls one of five results:
   - **Great +2** · **Success +1** · **Hold** (nothing happens) · **Notch −1** · **Shatter** (the weapon is lost and only worth 10 wen as scrap).
   The higher the grade, the higher the shatter chance. Hitting the green band improves the odds, and the bright centre halves the shatter chance. The odds are always shown on screen.
3. **The furnace spirit.** Every strike feeds it. After 10 strikes, your next **5 strikes are completely safe**: they cannot notch or shatter and cost no heat.
4. **Quench** whenever you dare go no further. The weapon's value grows fast with its grade (about ×1.55 per grade). At +10 a weapon becomes a named family blade: Dragon Spring, Vermilion Bird, Jade Moon or Thunder Edict.
5. **Hand in at dusk.** If the weapons you hand in meet the quota, you are paid what you made over it, plus a small stipend, and earn **merit**. Earn 1.5× the quota for extra merit. Miss it and the day counts as failed, and the weapons are taken without pay.

**Strongbox:** you can keep up to **3 finished weapons** instead of handing them in, and hand them in on a later, harder day. Quotas rise every day.

**Old Fang the peddler** visits every evening with charms and tools to buy with your silver, and a jar of sorghum wine that lowers tomorrow's quota. Silver also matters at home: bribes, gifts and journeys all cost money.

## Your family and the attractiveness setting

Before you begin you name your smith, your wife, your 16-year-old daughter and your 6-year-old son, and you choose the **attractiveness** of your wife and daughter from 1 to 5 (Plain, Pleasant, Pretty, Striking, Breathtaking).

The County Magistrate's son, **Gao Ying**, has a roving eye. The more beautiful your family, the sooner and harder trouble finds them:

| Highest attractiveness | What tends to happen |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 | A quiet life. At worst, a war levy. |
| 3 | Gossip and matchmakers. |
| 4 | Danger: Gao Ying takes an interest. |
| 5 | Disaster on your first leave: you come home to find your wife and daughter abducted, and your neighbour Granny Wang looking after your son. |

Defying the Gao family raises their grudge, which makes the next event worse. Protecting your family lowers it.

## Home leave and events

After each tour of duty you get **three days of leave** at home. Each day you choose one activity, and each one leaves a lasting mark:

- **Throw a family feast**: the furnace spirit starts half full next tour.
- **Rebuild the family bellows with your son**: the furnace's good band is permanently wider.
- **Mend Granny Wang's roof**: your neighbour starts to trust you, which opens up later choices.
- **Pray at the Furnace God's temple**: saves one shattering strike next tour.
- **Send your daughter to your sister in the hills**: hides her from unwanted attention.
- **Sleep late and rest your arms**: 2 extra strikes per weapon next tour.

On the second day, something happens, depending on how dangerous things have become:

- **The war levy**: pay Gao Ying's "levy", or refuse.
- **The matchmaker**: Gao Ying wants your daughter as his seventh concubine. Take the bride price, refuse, betroth her to the neighbour's grandson, or forge Gao Ying a +5 sword to make him forget her.
- **Market day**: Gao Ying grabs your wife or daughter in the market. Apologise, bribe his steward, show him General Meng's token (if you have enough merit), or knock him into the fish tub and flee with your family.
- **The abduction**: your family is taken to the Gao manor. You can thank Granny Wang, petition the county court (the magistrate is Gao Ying's father), forge a blade and storm the manor at night, ride to General Meng for help, or travel to the capital and beat the Grievance Drum.

Some choices are **forge challenges**: you get one special weapon and must reach a target grade (+5, +6, +8 or +10) for the plan to work.

## Stages

Where you forge depends on your choices:

| Stage | Overseer | How you get there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Army supply camp** | Sergeant Zhao | Where every story starts. |
| **Reed Marsh hideout** | Iron Ox, chief of the outlaws | Fleeing after a fight with Gao Ying, a successful raid or an escape. |
| **The Gao household armory** | Steward Qian | Accepting the magistrate's offer, or being caught in a raid. |
| **The Royal Arsenal** | Minister Gong | General Meng rescues your family and recommends you to the King. |

Each stage has its own quotas, its own leave events and its own way to end.

## Endings

There are eight endings. Some are happy, some are bitter, and one is a legend.

1. **Home with honour**: the war ends and you walk home through the willow gate.
2. **Bitter silver**: you took the bride price.
3. **The empty house**: your family never came home from the Gao manor.
4. **Outlaws of the Reed Marsh**: poor, hunted, and together.
5. **The gilded cage**: fed, warm, and never free.
6. **Royal Smith**: a good life in the capital.
7. **Smith Under Heaven**: forge the King's victory sword to +10 or better.
8. **Sent to the front**: too many failed days.

## Tips

- Quench early on cheap weapons and push your luck on the ones that are already high.
- Use the strongbox: a +7 blade kept for day 9 is worth more than handing it in on day 2.
- Merit is your safety net. Beat the quota by half to earn extra.
- Be kind to Granny Wang. She remembers.
- Silver matters at home too. Don't spend it all at Old Fang's.

## For developers

```
src/                 game source
  style.css          layout and look
  body.html          page markup (text comes from the language files)
  game.js            game logic and story
  canvas.js          anvil drawing, effects and sound
  lang/<code>.js     one text table per language
tools/build.py       builds web/ (the installable app) and dist/ (one-file build)
tools/check-i18n.js  checks every language against English
tools/make-icons.js  renders the icon PNGs from assets/icon.svg
electron/main.js     desktop wrapper
web/                 the built web app (what GitHub Pages serves)
```

```bash
python3 tools/build.py       # build web/ and dist/
node tools/check-i18n.js     # check the translations
npm install && npm start     # run the desktop app
npm run dist                 # build a desktop installer for this computer
```

**Adding a language:** copy `src/lang/en.js` to `src/lang/<code>.js` and translate the values, keeping every `{placeholder}`. Add the code to `LANGS` in `tools/build.py`, to `LANG_ORDER`, `LANG_NAMES` and `LOCALES` in `src/game.js`, and to the list in `tools/check-i18n.js`, then run the checker.

**Publishing:** every push to `main` rebuilds the web version on GitHub Pages. Pushing a version tag such as `v1.1.0` builds Windows, Mac, Linux and Android in GitHub Actions and attaches them to a release.

The game is a single page with no server and no tracking. Your progress is saved only on your own device.
